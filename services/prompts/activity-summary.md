You are given one hour of one person's activity at their computer:

1. A MEASURED TIMELINE — every session in the hour: the app, the window title, and when it ran.
2. The screen capture for the hour — OCR and accessibility text scraped from what was on their screen. Noisy, fragmentary, out of order.
3. Summaries of any AI coding-agent sessions they ran. These were written by the agent itself and are accurate.

List what they did in that hour, in time order, one paragraph per distinct activity.

WHAT TO COVER

Everything. Writing code is one activity, not the only one that counts. Reading documentation, watching a video, sitting in a meeting, replying on Slack, scrolling Reddit or X, reading the news, filling in a form, shopping, doing nothing — all of it was part of the hour and all of it belongs here.

Never leave an activity out because it looks unproductive, and never fold it into a work activity to make it look like work. If the timeline shows reddit.com, that is browsing Reddit — it is not "researching". If it shows netflix.com, that is watching Netflix — it is not part of the coding task that happened either side of it. Give browsing, reading and watching their own paragraphs, exactly as you would give a coding task its own.

Do not judge, rank or editorialise. No "productively", no "unfortunately", no "only", no "wasted". Say what happened.

Walk the MEASURED TIMELINE from its first entry to its last and account for ALL of it. Every app in that timeline must be covered by some paragraph. An activity in the timeline that gets no paragraph is a hole in the record. Do not split one continuous activity across paragraphs, and never repeat one you have already covered. Most hours have three or four activities. Rarely more than six.

HOW TO WRITE IT

Number each paragraph: "1. ", "2. ", "3. " — then the prose. No headings, no bullet lists.

After the number, the FIRST WORD is a past-tense verb naming the activity: Investigated, Built, Debugged, Shipped, Reviewed, Read, Watched, Scrolled, Replied, Designed, Refactored, Browsed, Tested, Deployed.

NEVER NAME THE PERSON — anywhere, not just at the start. The words "the developer", "the user", "they", "he", "she" must not appear in your answer. The reader IS the person; writing about them in the third person is wrong. Delete the subject and begin with the verb: not "The developer fixed X", just "Fixed X".

NO TIME. Do not write a clock time, a duration, or a minute count anywhere — not "09:15", not "twenty minutes", not "spent", not "briefly", not "most of the hour", not "the first half". Time is worked out separately and added for you afterwards. A time in your answer is an error.

Be concrete. Name what makes the activity identifiable: the video, the article, the site, the thread, the document, the feature, the file, the error, the PR, the tool. Say what came of it — what got fixed, built, decided, replied to, abandoned, or left hanging. Something that failed or led nowhere still happened; say that it did. If an activity produced nothing, say it produced nothing.

NUMBERS MUST COME FROM THE INPUT. Test counts, PR numbers, line counts — copy them only if they are actually written in the input. An invented number is worse than no number.

Under 300 words total.
